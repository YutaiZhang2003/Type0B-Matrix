# Direct workload counts through total level 15

These counts describe the current Python punctured-theta CCY pipeline and the direct physical NS–R–R PBW implementation, for the same sector and truncations as the saved machine-precision level-10 runs. They are computed from integer generating functions and the production loop bounds; no level-15 block, branching coefficient, Gram entry, or Ward value was evaluated. The counting pass takes approximately 0.1 seconds.

## CCY

The count includes both Virasoro copies, the current reuse under exchange of the two split Ramond edges, and the different descendant cutoff for each branching term:

\[
2\ell_1+\ell_L+\ell_R+2\ell_3
\le 2N-\left(4n_1^2+2n_2^2+2(n_2')^2+4n_3^2-\tfrac12\right).
\]

These are sums across series, whose numerical caches are cleared after each series. The largest single level-15 series has 13,056 requested coefficients, 1,788,190 nonterminal pole expansions, 18,869,296 stored child terms, and 138,210,492 pole additions. There are four series at this largest weighted cutoff, 30. The largest edge levels separately allowed are (15,30,30,15), not simultaneously.

The structural counts assume the scheduled branching prefactors and residues are nonzero. Exact zeros can reduce the executed graph, and zero series coefficients can reduce the scalar-product count. The count matches all available level-10 production counters exactly: series, pole expansions, terminal globals, numerical evaluations, templates, pole geometry, and pole-term additions. This establishes the counting logic against an actual completed run; it does not establish absence of additional exact-zero skips or numerical failures at level 15.

### How the pole additions are counted

For each coordinate, an accumulated weight shift is either zero or an integer at least two, because every recursion step shifts by rs with r>=2 and s>=1. Each shift/remaining-level pair satisfying the weighted budget occurs in the full scheduled graph. Its pole expansion is constructed once.

For a remaining-level vector, count the allowed outgoing (edge,r,s) triples. For a shift vector, count the incoming triples for which subtracting rs leaves an allowed shift, namely zero or an integer at least two. A nonterminal expansion is evaluated once per incoming triple, plus once if all shifts are zero (the requested root coefficient). Each evaluation adds every outgoing pole term. Thus the addition count is the sum of **(incoming count + root indicator) times outgoing count** over the shift/level lattice.

The counter convolves four small integer arrays carrying these counts and their products. It does not traverse billions of numerical terms. The stored-term count is only the outgoing count summed once per expansion, which explains why it differs from the numerical addition count.

Fusion-polynomial evaluations are not counted exactly: the production cache has a 32,768-entry LRU limit, so its misses depend on traversal order and numerical keys. Universal residue data, including the cached combination of A with the derivative of the pole position, are counted above. The detailed SL(2) polynomial arithmetic inside global seeds is also not counted as individual scalar operations.

## Direct physical PBW

The basis sizes are generated by

\[
\prod_{m\ge1}\frac{1+x^{2m-1}}{1-x^{2m}}
\quad\text{(NS, exponent counts twice the level)},\qquad
\prod_{m\ge1}\frac{1+y^m}{1-y^m}
\quad\text{(R, dimension per parity)}.
\]

They were checked against the actual production metadata enumerator at every level through 15, including both Ramond parities. No matrix elements were computed in that check.

| Largest R Gram matrix dimension, per parity | 232 | 1,472 | 6.34 |

For basis dimensions a,b,c on a physical level triple, the production code requests 4abc tensor entries (two eta forms, two parity sectors). It performs 2abc(a+b+c+1) scalar complex multiplications and 2[abc(a+b+c-2)-1] additions in the contractions, including the final dot products. These are algebraic dense-operation counts; NumPy/BLAS may organize their execution differently.

The unique Gram-entry count is the sum of the squares of all NS dimensions plus four times the sum of the squares of all R per-parity dimensions (two R edges and two parities). The matrices and inverses alone require 653,818,848 bytes, approximately 624 MiB, at level 15. This excludes all Python objects and Ward/mode-action caches.

Requested tensor entries are not counts of fresh Ward-recursion evaluations: some values are already cached and others trigger several recursive operations. Likewise, a Gram entry can involve many mode actions. These internal operation counts are not supplied by the basis generating functions. The sum of dimensions cubed is an inversion complexity proxy, not an exact FLOP count.

## What these counts imply for time

The level-10 CCY stage took 33.997 seconds for 87,397,160 pole additions, **including** seed/residue construction and other recursion overhead. Scaling that effective cost by the directly counted 7,402,804,416 additions gives **2,880 seconds, about 48 minutes**, for a pole-count-normalized level-15 CCY-stage estimate. This is not a full-pipeline measurement or a guaranteed upper bound. Many other CCY work items grow more slowly, whereas larger caches can lower throughput. Outer/middle branching, auxiliary computation, products and division must also be included in an end-to-end run.

For PBW, two nonnegative cost models were calibrated against the 506 per-coefficient time increments in the saved machine-precision level-10 log. Their inputs are the newly required Gram entries, requested tensor entries, and (in the richer model) the directly counted dense-contraction products and cubic matrix-size proxy. There is no fit to an exponential or power of the total level.

- An entry-count model gives approximately **1,106 seconds (18 minutes)** at level 15.
- A model also including matrix-size and contraction work gives approximately **2,373 seconds (40 minutes)**.

This 18–40 minute spread measures sensitivity to the cost model, not a confidence interval. The internal Ward/mode-action complexity and memory behavior may change with level. In particular, the cubic-size feature correlates with expensive Gram construction as well as inversion; its fitted coefficient is not a measured BLAS inversion rate. These operation-based estimates do not determine which full computation will finish first.
