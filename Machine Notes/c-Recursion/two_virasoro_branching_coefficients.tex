\begin{abstract}
The human note reduces the all-NS superconformal block to two ordinary
Virasoro blocks and leaves one quantity undetermined:
the normalized three-point coefficient of the
\(\Vir\oplus\Vir\) highest-weight vectors \(v_n\).
We use the free-field highest-vector prescription suggested by
arXiv:1111.2803, but every norm and three-point coefficient is evaluated with
the BPZ, tensor-product, and fixed-parity conventions of the human note.
Writing \(k=2n\in\mathbb Z\), the vector is a finite Fermi-sea product of the
modes \(\chi_r=\psi_r-i\eta_r\).  The factorized ratio \(l\) in the paper is
recorded only as a comparison; it is not identified with the human-note
three-point function.  The complete direct calculation at
\(n_i\in\{0,\pm\frac12\}\) is given below.  No Whittaker vector is used.
\end{abstract}

\section{NS free-field realization}

We first fix the realization in which the branching vector will be computed.
Set
\begin{equation}
 Q=b+b^{-1},\qquad
 c=\frac32+3Q^2,\qquad
 h(P)=\frac12\left(\frac{Q^2}{4}-P^2\right).
 \label{eq:free-field-parameters}
\end{equation}
All modes in this section are NS modes:
\(m\in\mathbb Z\) and \(r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12\).

\subsection{Oscillators and Fock vacuum}

Following the human note, the auxiliary Majorana fermion has modes
\(\psi_r\).  The free-field realization of the SCA uses a Heisenberg
oscillator \(c_m^\epsilon\), a physical NS fermion \(\eta_r^\epsilon\), and
a bosonic zero mode \(\mathcal P\).
Here \(\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\) labels two \emph{alternative reflected
realizations} of the same SCA module; the \(+\) and \(-\) oscillators are not
two simultaneous free fields.  Their nonzero brackets are
\begin{align}
 [c_m^\epsilon,c_n^\epsilon]&=m\delta_{m+n,0},
 &\{\eta_r^\epsilon,\eta_s^\epsilon\}&=\delta_{r+s,0},
 &\{\psi_r,\psi_s\}&=\delta_{r+s,0},
 \label{eq:free-field-algebra}\\
 [\mathcal P,c_m^\epsilon]&=[\mathcal P,\eta_r^\epsilon]=0,
 &[\psi_r,c_m^\epsilon]&=0,
 &\{\psi_r,\eta_s^\epsilon\}&=0.
 \notag
\end{align}
The auxiliary BPZ convention inherited from the human note is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_r^\dagger=-\psi_{-r}.
 \label{eq:psi-BPZ}
\end{equation}

Let \(\one_{\Ff}\) be annihilated by \(\psi_r\) for \(r>0\), and let
\(\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon\) be annihilated by \(c_m^\epsilon\) and
\(\eta_r^\epsilon\) for \(m,r>0\), with
\(\mathcal P\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon=P\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon\).  We write
\begin{equation}
 \lvert\mathrm{vac};P\rangle
 :=\one_{\Ff}\otimes\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon.
 \label{eq:free-field-vacuum}
\end{equation}
Thus the tensor-product module is the span of arbitrary finite products of
the negative modes \(\psi_{-r}\), \(c_{-m}^\epsilon\), and
\(\eta_{-r}^\epsilon\) acting on
\(\lvert\mathrm{vac};P\rangle\).

\subsection{SCA generators}

In the realization labelled by \(\epsilon\), the abstract SCA generators act
for \(m\ne0\) as
\begin{align}
 L_m
 &=\frac12\sum_{j\ne0,m}c_j^\epsilon c_{m-j}^\epsilon
  +\frac12\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}
       \left(r-\frac m2\right)
       \eta_{m-r}^\epsilon\eta_r^\epsilon
  +\frac i2(Qm+2\epsilon\mathcal P)c_m^\epsilon,
 \notag\\
 G_r
 &=\sum_{m\ne0}c_m^\epsilon\eta_{r-m}^\epsilon
   +i(Qr+\epsilon\mathcal P)\eta_r^\epsilon,
 \label{eq:free-field-SCA}\\
 L_0
 &=\sum_{m>0}c_{-m}^\epsilon c_m^\epsilon
   +\sum_{r>0}r\eta_{-r}^\epsilon\eta_r^\epsilon
   +\frac12\left(\frac{Q^2}{4}-\mathcal P^2\right).
 \notag
\end{align}
These formulas obey the \(\mathcal N=1\) SCA relations at the central charge
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:free-field-parameters}, and
\(L_0\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon=h(P)\lvert P\rangle_\epsilon\).
The realization \(\epsilon=-1\) is used for \(k>0\), and
\(\epsilon=+1\) for \(k<0\).  They are related by the super-Liouville
reflection operator.

It is now meaningful to define the isotropic fermionic modes
\begin{equation}
 \chi_r^\epsilon:=\psi_r-i\eta_r^\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \{\chi_r^\epsilon,\chi_s^\epsilon\}=0.
 \label{eq:chi-definition}
\end{equation}
The vanishing anticommutator is the reason that the highest vector below is a
finite Fermi-sea product.

\subsection{The commuting Virasoro pair}

For completeness, define the auxiliary-fermion stress tensor and the mixed
bilinear
\begin{equation}
 L_n^{\Ff}=\frac12\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}
 r:\psi_{n-r}\psi_r:,
 \qquad
 U_n=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}\psi_{n-r}G_r.
 \label{eq:LF-U}
\end{equation}
The two commuting Virasoro generators used in the human note are
\begin{align}
 L_n^{(1)}
 &=\frac{b^{-1}}{b^{-1}-b}L_n
   -\frac{b^{-1}+2b}{b^{-1}-b}L_n^{\Ff}
   +\frac{1}{b^{-1}-b}U_n,
 \notag\\
 L_n^{(2)}
 &=\frac{b}{b-b^{-1}}L_n
   -\frac{b+2b^{-1}}{b-b^{-1}}L_n^{\Ff}
   +\frac{1}{b-b^{-1}}U_n.
 \label{eq:two-Vir-generators}
\end{align}
They obey
\begin{equation}
 [L_m^{(1)},L_n^{(2)}]=0,
 \qquad
 c^{(i)}=1+6\bigl(Q^{(i)}\bigr)^2,
 \qquad
 Q^{(i)}=b^{(i)}+\bigl(b^{(i)}\bigr)^{-1},
 \label{eq:two-Vir-central-charges}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 b^{(1)}=\frac{2b}{\sqrt{2-2b^2}},
 \qquad
 \bigl(b^{(2)}\bigr)^{-1}
 =\frac{2b^{-1}}{\sqrt{2-2b^{-2}}}.
 \label{eq:two-Vir-couplings}
\end{equation}
This finishes the algebraic setup.  Every branching vector computed below is
an explicit vector in the Fock space of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:free-field-algebra}--\eqref{eq:free-field-vacuum}.

\section{The quantity left open in the human note}

The human note uses
\begin{equation}
 \M_{\mathrm{NS}}(h)
 =\V_{\Ff_{1/2}}\otimes\V_{\SCA_{1/2}}(h),
 \qquad
 h=\frac12\left(\frac{Q^2}{4}-P^2\right),
 \qquad Q=b+b^{-1},
 \label{eq:module}
\end{equation}
with the auxiliary free-fermion factor first.  The branching rule is
\begin{equation}
 \M_{\mathrm{NS}}(h)
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{n\in\frac12\mathbb Z}
 \V_{c^{(1)}}(h_n^{(1)})\otimes
 \V_{c^{(2)}}(h_n^{(2)}),
 \qquad
 h_n^{(1)}+h_n^{(2)}=h+2n^2.
 \label{eq:branching}
\end{equation}
The corresponding highest-weight vector is denoted by \(v_n\), and its
parity is \(2n\pmod2\).

For the ordered trinion used throughout the human note, slot \(1\) is the
BPZ-conjugate state at infinity, slot \(2\) is inserted at \(1\), and slot
\(3\) is the ket at \(0\).  In precisely this order the human note defines
\begin{equation}
 B_a(h_1,h_2,h_3;n_1,n_2,n_3)
 =\frac{
  \hatrho_a(v_{1,n_1},v_{2,n_2},v_{3,n_3})
 }{
  \lVert v_{1,n_1}\rVert
  \lVert v_{2,n_2}\rVert
  \lVert v_{3,n_3}\rVert
 },
 \qquad
 a=2(n_1+n_2+n_3)\pmod2.
 \label{eq:B-definition}
\end{equation}
Only \(B_a^2\) enters the conformal-block decomposition.  Directly from the
human definition,
\begin{equation}
 \bigl[B_a(h_1,h_2,h_3;n_1,n_2,n_3)\bigr]^2
 =\frac{\hatrho_a(v_{1,n_1},v_{2,n_2},v_{3,n_3})^2}
 {N_{k_1}(P_1)N_{k_2}(P_2)N_{k_3}(P_3)},
 \qquad
 k_i=2n_i,
 \qquad
 N_{k_i}(P_i)=\lVert v_{i,n_i}\rVert^2.
 \label{eq:B-square-target}
\end{equation}

No reversal of the labels \(1\leftrightarrow3\) is made in this note.  Other
slot orders must be obtained with the M\"obius and Koszul conventions already
fixed in the human note.

\section{Highest vector from the free-field realization}

\subsection{The Fermi-sea vector}

Set \(k=2n\in\mathbb Z\).  For \(k\ne0\), the prescription is
\begin{equation}
 |P,k\rangle
 =\Omega_k(P)
 \prod_{j=1}^{|k|}
 \chi_{-(2j-1)/2}^{-\operatorname{sgn}(k)}
 |\mathrm{vac};P\rangle,
 \qquad
 |P,0\rangle=|\mathrm{vac};P\rangle.
 \label{eq:free-field-vector}
\end{equation}
The product is ordered with increasing \(j\) from left to right.  Its total
level is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=1}^{|k|}\frac{2j-1}{2}=\frac{k^2}{2}=2n^2,
 \label{eq:Fermi-level}
\end{equation}
which is precisely the shift in Eq.~\eqref{eq:branching}.

The factor \(\Omega_k(P)\) is fixed by the standard SCA-PBW normalization
\begin{equation}
 |P,k\rangle
 =\left(G_{-1/2}^{k^2}+\text{terms of lower degree in }G\right)|P\rangle.
 \label{eq:PBW-normalization}
\end{equation}
This fixes the vector, including its scale, without referring to a Whittaker
state.

The commutators quoted in the literature are
\begin{align}
 [L_m^{(1)}+L_m^{(2)},\chi_r^\epsilon]
 &=-\left(\frac m2+r\right)\chi_{m+r}^\epsilon,
 \notag\\
 [bL_m^{(1)}+b^{-1}L_m^{(2)},\chi_r^\epsilon]
 &=-\left((m+r)Q+\epsilon\mathcal P\right)
      \chi_{m+r}^\epsilon
   +i\sum_{j\ne0}c_j^\epsilon\chi_{m+r-j}^\epsilon.
 \label{eq:chi-commutators}
\end{align}
They prove directly that Eq.~\eqref{eq:free-field-vector} is killed by both
positive Virasoro algebras.  The two weights are
\begin{align}
 h_n^{(1)}
 &=\frac14(Q^{(1)})^2
  -\left(\frac{P}{\sqrt{2-2b^2}}+nb^{(1)}\right)^2,
 \notag\\
 h_n^{(2)}
 &=\frac14(Q^{(2)})^2
  -\left(\frac{P}{\sqrt{2-2b^{-2}}}+n(b^{(2)})^{-1}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:branch-weights}
\end{align}

The first nontrivial vectors, already converted back to the human note's SCA
and auxiliary-fermion modes, are
\begin{align}
 |P,1\rangle
 &=\left[G_{-1/2}+\left(\frac Q2+P\right)\psi_{-1/2}\right]|P,0\rangle,
 \notag\\
 |P,-1\rangle
 &=\left[G_{-1/2}+\left(\frac Q2-P\right)\psi_{-1/2}\right]|P,0\rangle.
 \label{eq:k-one-vectors}
\end{align}
They are the human-note vectors \(v_{1/2}\) and \(v_{-1/2}\).

\section{Factorized three-point numerator}

\subsection{Dictionary for an ordered trinion}

Parameterize the inserted NS weight by
\begin{equation}
 h_2=\frac12\alpha_2(Q-\alpha_2),
 \qquad
 \alpha_2=\frac Q2+P_2.
 \label{eq:alpha-dictionary}
\end{equation}
For the ordered matrix element
\begin{equation}
 \langle P_1,k_1|\mathbb V_{\alpha_2}^{(k_2)}(1)|P_3,k_3\rangle,
 \label{eq:ordered-matrix-element}
\end{equation}
the state-field correspondence identifies
\(\mathbb V_{\alpha_2}^{(k_2)}\) with the same highest vector
\(|P_2,k_2\rangle\) used on the middle leg.  The normalized ratio in
arXiv:1111.2803 is
\begin{equation}
 l(\alpha,m\mid P',k',P,k)
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac{\langle P',k'|\mathbb V_\alpha^{(m)}|P,k\rangle}
 {\langle P'|\mathbb V_\alpha^{(0)}|P\rangle},
 &m+k+k'\text{ even},\\[1.1em]
 \displaystyle
 \frac{\langle P',k'|\mathbb V_\alpha^{(m)}|P,k\rangle}
 {\langle P'|\mathbb V_\alpha^{(\pm1)}|P\rangle},
 &m+k+k'\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:l-definition}
\end{equation}
The corresponding primary denominators have the same numerical normalization
as the human note:
\(\rho_0(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)=1\) in the even case and
\(\rho_1(\phi_1,G_{-1/2}\phi_2,\phi_3)=1\) in the odd case.  Define the
literature ratio in the human slot labels by
\begin{equation}
 L_a^{\mathrm{lit}}(1,2,3)
 :=l(\alpha_2,k_2\mid P_1,k_1,P_3,k_3),
 \qquad
 a=k_1+k_2+k_3\pmod2.
 \label{eq:L-lit-definition}
\end{equation}
Matching the two primary denominators does \emph{not} by itself prove
\begin{equation}
 T_a^{\mathrm H}=L_a^{\mathrm{lit}}(1,2,3).
 \label{eq:T-versus-L-question}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:T-versus-L-question} is a convention-sensitive statement
about the complete highest vectors.  It must also match the auxiliary
fermion BPZ sign, the fixed-parity conversion in the human note, and the
state--field map for the middle vector.  This is the central audit below.

\subsection{The product formula}

For \(\sigma,\tau\in\{+1,-1\}\), define
\begin{equation}
 x_{\sigma\tau}:=\alpha+\sigma P'+\tau P,
 \qquad
 r_{\sigma\tau}:=\frac{m+\sigma k'+\tau k}{2}.
 \label{eq:x-r}
\end{equation}
For a nonnegative integer \(r\), set
\begin{align}
 \se(x,r)
 &=2^{-r^2/2}
 \prod_{\substack{i,j\ge1,\ i+j\le2r\\i+j\equiv0\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
 \left[x+(i-1)b+(j-1)b^{-1}\right],
 \notag\\
 \so(x,r)
 &=2^{-r(r+1)/2}
 \prod_{\substack{i,j\ge1,\ i+j\le2r+1\\i+j\equiv1\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
 \left[x+(i-1)b+(j-1)b^{-1}\right].
 \label{eq:s-positive}
\end{align}
For negative integer \(r\), extend them by
\begin{equation}
 \se(x,r)=(-1)^r\se(Q-x,-r),
 \qquad
 \so(x,r)=\so(Q-x,-r).
 \label{eq:s-negative}
\end{equation}
Finally define
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Int}(x):=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\lfloor|x|\rfloor.
 \label{eq:Int}
\end{equation}

The literature ratio is then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 l(\alpha,m\mid P',k',P,k)
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \prod_{\sigma,\tau=\pm1}
 \se\left(x_{\sigma\tau},r_{\sigma\tau}\right),
 &m+k+k'\text{ even},\\[1.2em]
 \displaystyle
 \prod_{\sigma,\tau=\pm1}
 \so\left(x_{\sigma\tau},
 \operatorname{Int}(r_{\sigma\tau})\right),
 &m+k+k'\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}}
 \label{eq:l-product}
\end{equation}
This is the factorized matrix-element ratio of arXiv:1111.2803.  It becomes
the desired human-note numerator only after
Eq.~\eqref{eq:T-versus-L-question}, including any convention factor, has
been established.

\subsection{Endpoint order and the first convention check}

The fixed-parity trilinear form used in the human note obeys the endpoint
reflection rule
\begin{equation}
 \hatrho_a(x_1,x_2,x_3)
 =(-1)^{|x_2|(|x_1|+|x_3|)}
 \hatrho_a(x_3,x_2,x_1),
 \label{eq:human-reflection}
\end{equation}
with the weights and labels in slots (1) and (3) exchanged on the
right-hand side.  For the branching primaries this sign is
\((-1)^{k_2(k_1+k_3)}\).  Thus the reversed expression
\(\hatrho_a(v_{3,n_3},v_{2,n_2},v_{1,n_1})\), used in an earlier version of
this note, is not the definition in the human note and cannot be substituted
without this reflection sign.

Already at \(n_i=0,\pm\tfrac12\), one must expand the explicit vectors
\begin{equation}
 v_{i,\epsilon_i/2}
 =G_{-1/2}\phi_i+a_i \psi_{-1/2}\phi_i,
 \qquad
 a_i:=\frac Q2+\epsilon_iP_i,
 \qquad \epsilon_i=\pm1,
 \label{eq:level-half-vectors-a}
\end{equation}
inside the tensor-product trilinear form of the human note.  Its auxiliary
BPZ convention gives
\begin{equation}
 \rho^{\Ff}(\psi\one,\psi\one,\one)=-1,
 \qquad
 \rho^{\Ff}(\psi\one,\one,\psi\one)=-1,
 \qquad
 \rho^{\Ff}(\one,\psi\one,\psi\one)=+1.
 \label{eq:fermion-level-half-rhos}
\end{equation}
Using the human note's fixed-parity component table then gives
\begin{align}
 T_0^{\mathrm H}[\epsilon_1/2,\epsilon_2/2,0]
 &=-\frac12\left[
  \left(\frac Q2+\epsilon_1P_1+\epsilon_2P_2\right)^2-P_3^2
 \right],
 \notag\\
 T_0^{\mathrm H}[\epsilon_1/2,0,\epsilon_3/2]
 &=\frac12\left[
  P_2^2-\left(\frac Q2+\epsilon_1P_1+\epsilon_3P_3\right)^2
 \right],
 \notag\\
 T_0^{\mathrm H}[0,\epsilon_2/2,\epsilon_3/2]
 &=\frac12\left[
  \left(\frac Q2+\epsilon_2P_2+\epsilon_3P_3\right)^2-P_1^2
 \right].
 \label{eq:human-two-branch-direct}
\end{align}
The product formula for \(L^{\mathrm{lit}}\) has the same last two entries
but the opposite sign in the first entry.  Equivalently, on these cases,
\begin{equation}
 T_a^{\mathrm H}=(-1)^{k_1k_2}L_a^{\mathrm{lit}}.
 \label{eq:low-level-convention-factor}
\end{equation}
This establishes a genuine convention factor at the first nontrivial level.
Whether Eq.~\eqref{eq:low-level-convention-factor} is the complete all-level
translation must be checked with an explicit
\(|k|=2\) vector; it is not assumed below for an unsquared coefficient.

\section{Norm from the same formula}

Take the inserted field to be the identity:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=0,
 \qquad m=0,
 \qquad P'=P,
 \qquad k'=k.
 \label{eq:norm-specialization}
\end{equation}
Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-definition} becomes the norm, while the four factors in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-product} reduce to
\begin{equation}
 (x_{++},r_{++})=(2P,k),
 \quad
 (x_{+-},r_{+-})=(0,0),
 \quad
 (x_{-+},r_{-+})=(0,0),
 \quad
 (x_{--},r_{--})=(-2P,-k).
 \label{eq:norm-four-factors}
\end{equation}
Since \(\se(x,0)=1\), we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 N_k(P)
 =\langle P,k|P,k\rangle
 =\se(2P,k)\se(-2P,-k).}
 \label{eq:norm-master}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for \(p\ge0\),
\begin{align}
 N_p(P)&=(-1)^p\se(2P,p)\se(Q+2P,p),
 \notag\\
 N_{-p}(P)&=(-1)^p\se(Q-2P,p)\se(-2P,p).
 \label{eq:norm-piecewise}
\end{align}

At level \(1/2\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:k-one-vectors} gives an independent direct
check.  Using
\begin{equation}
 \langle G_{-1/2}\phi,G_{-1/2}\phi\rangle=2h,
 \qquad
 \langle \psi_{-1/2}\one,\psi_{-1/2}\one\rangle=-1,
 \label{eq:level-half-inner-products}
\end{equation}
and vanishing mixed inner product, one finds
\begin{align}
 N_1(P)
 &=2h-\left(\frac Q2+P\right)^2
 =-P(Q+2P),
 \notag\\
 N_{-1}(P)
 &=2h-\left(\frac Q2-P\right)^2
 =P(Q-2P).
 \label{eq:level-half-norms}
\end{align}
These agree with Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm-master}, because
\(\se(x,1)=x/\sqrt2\).  The same computation also gives
\begin{equation}
 \langle P,1|P,-1\rangle=0.
 \label{eq:level-half-orthogonality}
\end{equation}

\section{Squared coefficient obtained from the literature ratio}

The product formula and the norm determine the following
literature-normalized candidate:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal B_a^{\mathrm{lit}}
 (P_1,P_2,P_3;k_1,k_2,k_3)
 =\frac{
 l\left(\frac Q2+P_2,k_2\mid P_1,k_1,P_3,k_3\right)^2
 }{
 N_{k_1}(P_1)N_{k_2}(P_2)N_{k_3}(P_3)
 },}
 \label{eq:B-final}
\end{equation}
where \(l\) is Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-product}, \(N\) is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:norm-master}, and
\begin{equation}
 k_i=2n_i,
 \qquad
 a=k_1+k_2+k_3\pmod2.
 \label{eq:final-dictionary}
\end{equation}
At the cases checked in Eq.~\eqref{eq:human-two-branch-direct}, the
translation is a sign and hence
\(\mathcal B_a^{\mathrm{lit}}=\mathcal B_a\).  The all-level identification
requires proving that the translation between \(T_a^{\mathrm H}\) and
\(L_a^{\mathrm{lit}}\) remains a pure sign for the explicit higher branching
vectors.

If an unsquared \(B_a\) is desired, one must choose square roots of the three
complex Shapovalov norms, fix an overall phase for every branch vector, and
include the human-to-literature trilinear convention factor.  Principal
square roots alone do not define the human-note coefficient.

\section{Low-level values}

The product formula immediately gives
\begin{equation}
 l(\alpha,0\mid P',0,P,0)=1,
 \qquad
 l(\alpha,1\mid P',0,P,0)=1.
 \label{eq:base-l}
\end{equation}
The first is the even primary normalization; the second is the odd
normalization.

With one \(k=1\) branch vector and the other two labels zero,
\begin{equation}
 l(\alpha,0\mid P',0,P,1)
 =l(\alpha,0\mid P',1,P,0)=1.
 \label{eq:one-branch-l}
\end{equation}
Consequently the corresponding squared normalized coefficient is simply the
inverse norm of that leg.

With two \(k=1\) vectors, the even numerators are
\begin{align}
 l(\alpha,0\mid P',1,P,1)
 &=\frac12(\alpha+P'+P)(\alpha-P'-P-Q),
 \notag\\
 l(\alpha,1\mid P',1,P,0)
 &=\frac12(\alpha+P'+P)(\alpha+P'-P),
 \notag\\
 l(\alpha,1\mid P',0,P,1)
 &=\frac12(\alpha+P'+P)(\alpha-P'+P).
 \label{eq:two-branch-l}
\end{align}
These are the first nontrivial polynomial checks to reproduce directly from
the human-note Ward identities.

For example,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_0^{\mathrm{lit}}(P_1,P_2,P_3;1,0,1)
 =\frac{
  (\alpha_2+P_3+P_1)^2
  (\alpha_2-P_3-P_1-Q)^2
 }{
  4N_1(P_3)N_1(P_1)
 },
 \qquad \alpha_2=\frac Q2+P_2.
 \label{eq:B-example}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Complete unpacking for

\section{Constructive physical-\texorpdfstring{\(L_1\)}{L1} action:
NS sector}
\label{sec:constructive-physical-L1-NS}

This section determines the action of the \emph{physical} NS Virasoro mode
\(L_1\) on every \(\Vir^{(1)}\oplus\Vir^{(2)}\) branching primary.  The
auxiliary Majorana fermion is denoted by \(\psi\), the physical free-field
fermion by \(\eta\), and all fermionic modes are in
\(\mathbb Z+\frac12\).

\subsection{The embedded generators, with the \texorpdfstring{\(\psi G\)}{psi G}
term displayed}

Set
\begin{equation}
 \delta_b=b^{-1}-b,
 \qquad
 U_m=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}\psi_{m-r}G_r.
 \label{eq:NS-U-definition}
\end{equation}
For \(m\ne0\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-Vir-generators} can be written as
\begin{align}
 L_m^{(1)}
 &=\frac{b^{-1}}{\delta_b}L_m
   -\frac{b^{-1}+2b}{\delta_b}L_m^{\Ff}
   +\frac1{\delta_b}U_m,
 \notag\\
 L_m^{(2)}
 &=-\frac b{\delta_b}L_m
   +\frac{b+2b^{-1}}{\delta_b}L_m^{\Ff}
   -\frac1{\delta_b}U_m.
 \label{eq:NS-two-Vir-U-explicit}
\end{align}
Thus neither embedded generator separately omits the \(\psi G\) term.  Two
linear combinations that will be used below are the full operator
identities
\begin{align}
 L_m^{(1)}+L_m^{(2)}
 &=L_m+L_m^{\Ff},
 \label{eq:NS-two-Vir-sum}\\
 bL_m^{(1)}+b^{-1}L_m^{(2)}
 &=2Q L_m^{\Ff}-U_m.
 \label{eq:NS-two-Vir-weighted-sum}
\end{align}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-two-Vir-sum} the two \(U_m\) coefficients cancel;
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-two-Vir-weighted-sum} the \(U_m\) term remains.  This is
the only cancellation used in the proof.

Let \(v_n\), \(n\in\frac12\mathbb Z\), be an NS branching primary.  Since
both positive embedded modes annihilate it, Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-two-Vir-sum}
gives
\begin{equation}
 (L_m+L_m^{\Ff})v_n=0,
 \qquad
 L_mv_n=-L_m^{\Ff}v_n,
 \qquad m>0.
 \label{eq:NS-physical-L-on-branch-primary}
\end{equation}
The \(\psi G\) term has not been discarded: it has canceled between the two
embedded generators before Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-physical-L-on-branch-primary}
is applied.

\subsection{Complex-fermion charge and the pair-field identity}

Choose the reflected realization appropriate to the sign of \(n\), and set
\begin{equation}
 \chi_r=\psi_r-i\eta_r,
 \qquad
 \bar\chi_r=\psi_r+i\eta_r,
 \qquad
 \Psi_r=\frac{\chi_r}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \Psi_r^*=\frac{\bar\chi_r}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:NS-complex-fermion}
\end{equation}
For \(n\ne0\), put \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(n)\).  The current is
oriented branchwise so that the fermion appearing in the finite sea has
charge \(\sigma\):
\begin{equation}
 [J_m,J_k]=m\delta_{m+k,0},
 \qquad
 [J_0,\Psi_r]=\sigma\Psi_r,
 \qquad
 [J_0,\Psi_r^*]=-\sigma\Psi_r^*,
 \qquad
 J_0v_n=2n\,v_n.
 \label{eq:NS-current-charge}
\end{equation}
For the positive branch this is
\(J(z)=:\!\Psi(z)\Psi^*(z)\!:\).  For the negative reflected branch its
negative is used.  This convention keeps the finite-sea fermion equal to
\(\Psi\), while assigning the total charge \(q=2n\).

The finite product in Eq.~\eqref{eq:free-field-vector}, equivalently
Eq.~(4.22) of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02568}{arXiv:1608.02568}, is exactly a
charged complex-fermion vacuum:
\begin{equation}
 v_n={\cal N}_n|q=2n;P\rangle,
 \qquad
 J_m|q;P\rangle=0\quad(m>0).
 \label{eq:NS-v-as-charged-vacuum}
\end{equation}
Its enlarged-module onset level is
\begin{equation}
 N_n=2n^2=\frac{q^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:NS-branch-onset}
\end{equation}

Let \(L_m^\eta\) denote the physical-fermion stress tensor.  The vector
\(v_n\) contains no physical-boson oscillator, so the bosonic terms in the
positive physical mode annihilate it and
\begin{equation}
 L_1v_n=L_1^\eta v_n.
\end{equation}
Combining this with Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-physical-L-on-branch-primary} gives
\begin{equation}
 L_1v_n=-\frac12(L_1^{\Ff}-L_1^\eta)v_n.
 \label{eq:NS-L1-stress-difference}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-complex-fermion} gives
\begin{equation}
 L_m^{\Ff}-L_m^\eta
 =\frac14\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}r\left(
 :\!\chi_{m-r}\chi_r\!:
 +:\!\bar\chi_{m-r}\bar\chi_r\!:
 \right).
 \label{eq:NS-stress-difference-pairs}
\end{equation}

The finite sea is made from \(\Psi\), so its conjugate removes two sea
fermions.  Define the opposite-charge spin-two field
\begin{equation}
 {\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z)
 =:\!\Psi^*(z)\partial\Psi^*(z)\!:
 =\sum_m{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,m}z^{-m-2}.
 \label{eq:NS-pair-fields}
\end{equation}
It has current charge \(-2\sigma\).  Antisymmetry implies
\begin{equation}
 {\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,m}
 =-\sum_r r:\!\Psi^*_{m-r}\Psi^*_r\!:.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-stress-difference} and
\eqref{eq:NS-stress-difference-pairs} therefore give
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1v_n=\frac14{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,1}v_n,
 \qquad \sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(n).}
 \label{eq:NS-L1-pair-field}
\end{equation}

The \(\chi\chi\) term has charge \(+2\sigma\) and would send \(q\) to
\(q+2\sigma\), increasing \(|q|\) by two.  Its lowest possible energy is
larger than the available energy \(q^2/2-1\), so it annihilates \(v_n\).
The \(\bar\chi\bar\chi\) term has charge \(-2\sigma\) and sends \(q\) to
\(q-2\sigma\).  The excitation level above the new charged vacuum is
\begin{equation}
 d_n=\frac{q^2}{2}-1-\frac{(|q|-2)^2}{2}
 =2|q|-3=4|n|-3.
 \label{eq:NS-L1-relative-level}
\end{equation}
For \(n=0,\pm\frac12\), no nonnegative target level is available and
\begin{equation}
 L_1v_0=L_1v_{1/2}=L_1v_{-1/2}=0.
 \label{eq:NS-L1-lowest-zero}
\end{equation}
For \(|n|\geq1\), the charge and level argument selects the adjacent charged
vacuum and the relative level.  The triangular-filtration argument below
upgrades this to the double-Virasoro support statement
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1v_n\in
 \operatorname{span}\left\{
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}v_{n-\operatorname{sgn}(n)}:
 |A|+|B|=4|n|-3
 \right\}.}
 \label{eq:NS-L1-selection}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Explicit Heisenberg descendant and normalization}

Normalize a charge-\(\alpha\) Heisenberg vertex by
\begin{equation}
 V_\alpha(z)|q\rangle
 =z^{\alpha q}
 \exp\left(\alpha\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{J_{-m}}m z^m\right)
 |q+\alpha\rangle
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{d\geq0}H_d^{(\alpha)}(J_-)z^d
 =\exp\left(\alpha\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{J_{-m}}m z^m\right),
 \qquad
 dH_d^{(\alpha)}
 =\alpha\sum_{m=1}^dJ_{-m}H_{d-m}^{(\alpha)}.
 \label{eq:NS-Heisenberg-polynomials}
\end{equation}
The mode \({\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,1}\) has vertex charge
\(-2\sigma\) and selects the coefficient of \(z^{-3}\), so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-pair-field} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1v_n=\kappa_n^{\mathrm{NS}}
 H_{4|n|-3}^{(-2\operatorname{sgn}(n))}(J_-)
 v_{n-\operatorname{sgn}(n)},
 \qquad |n|\geq1.}
 \label{eq:NS-L1-Heisenberg-construction}
\end{equation}

The scale can be fixed without a Ward-identity calculation.  For
\(k=2n>0\), choose the canonical charged vacuum
\begin{equation}
 |q=k\rangle
 =\Psi_{-k+1/2}\Psi_{-k+3/2}\cdots\Psi_{-1/2}|0\rangle.
\end{equation}
Reversing this order to the convention in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:free-field-vector}, and using \(\chi=\sqrt2\Psi\), gives
\begin{equation}
 {\cal N}_{k/2}
 =2^{k/2}(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\Omega_k(P),
 \qquad
 \frac{{\cal N}_{k/2}}{{\cal N}_{(k-2)/2}}
 =-2\frac{\Omega_k(P)}{\Omega_{k-2}(P)}.
\end{equation}
Choose the pair-field cocycle so that
\({\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma=V_{-2\sigma}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \kappa_n^{\mathrm{NS}}
 =-\frac12
 \frac{\Omega_{2n}(P)}
      {\Omega_{2n-2\operatorname{sgn}(n)}(P)}.}
 \label{eq:NS-kappa}
\end{equation}
The sign in Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-kappa} is checked independently by the first
nonzero example below.

\subsection{Triangular \texorpdfstring{\(J_0\)}{J0} filtration and support
theorem}

The current zero mode does not commute with the two embedded Virasoro
algebras.  What is useful is a weaker, one-sided statement.  Fix one
transition \(n\to n-\sigma\), \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(n)\), and keep
the reflected realization selected by the source label.  Put
\begin{equation}
 \widehat J_0=\sigma J_0,
 \qquad
 [\widehat J_0,\chi_r]=\chi_r.
 \label{eq:NS-outward-current}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\widehat J_0\) measures charge in the outward direction of the
source Fermi sea.

For \(m\ne0\), define
\begin{equation}
 S_m=L_m^{(1)}+L_m^{(2)},
 \qquad
 K_m=bL_m^{(1)}+b^{-1}L_m^{(2)}.
\end{equation}
The first combination is the total stress tensor
\(S_m=L_m+L_m^{\Ff}\), so it preserves the complex-fermion charge.  Direct
substitution of the free-field supercurrent into
\(K_m=2QL_m^{\Ff}-U_m\) gives
\begin{equation}
 K_m=K_m^{(0)}+\frac Q2X_m^{(+2)},
 \qquad
 X_m^{(+2)}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z+\frac12}
 r:\!\chi_{m-r}\chi_r\!:,
 \label{eq:NS-triangular-K}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 [\widehat J_0,S_m]=[\widehat J_0,K_m^{(0)}]=0,
 \qquad
 [\widehat J_0,X_m^{(+2)}]=2X_m^{(+2)}.
 \label{eq:NS-triangular-charges}
\end{equation}
There is no charge-lowering term.  To see this directly, the
\(c_j\)-dependent and momentum-dependent parts of \(U_m\) are modes of
the neutral bilinear \(:\!\psi\eta\!:\).  The remaining part is
\begin{align}
 2QL_m^{\Ff}-iQ\sum_r r:\!\psi_{m-r}\eta_r\!:
 & =Q\sum_r r:\!\psi_{m-r}(\psi_r-i\eta_r)\!:
 \notag\\
 & =\frac Q2\sum_r r:\!
 \bigl(\bar\chi_{m-r}\chi_r+\chi_{m-r}\chi_r\bigr)\!:.
 \label{eq:NS-one-sided-charge-derivation}
\end{align}
The first term on the last line is neutral and the second is
\(X_m^{(+2)}\).  Inverting the two linear combinations gives, for
\(\delta_b=b^{-1}-b\),
\begin{equation}
 L_m^{(1)}=\ell_m^{(1)}-\frac{Q}{2\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)},
 \qquad
 L_m^{(2)}=\ell_m^{(2)}+\frac{Q}{2\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)},
 \label{eq:NS-triangular-L12}
\end{equation}
with both \(\ell_m^{(i)}\) charge preserving.  Hence every negative
double-Virasoro mode preserves \(\widehat J_0\)-charge or raises it by two.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 [\widehat J_0,L_m^{(1)}]
 =-\frac{Q}{\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)},
 \qquad
 [\widehat J_0,L_m^{(2)}]
 =+\frac{Q}{\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)}.
 \label{eq:NS-actual-J0-commutators}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\widehat J_0\) neither commutes with the individual
Virasoro generators nor exchanges the two algebras.

Let
\begin{equation}
 v_-=v_{n-\sigma},
 \qquad
 q_-=2|n|-2,
 \qquad
 d=4|n|-3=2q_-+1.
\end{equation}
Denote by \({\cal D}_{q_-}[d]\) the level-\(d\) double-Virasoro descendant
space generated from \(v_-\), and by \({\cal F}_{q_-}[d]\) the
charge-\(q_-\), level-\(d\) free-field space:
\begin{align}
 {\cal D}_{q_-}[d]
 &={\rm span}\left\{
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}v_-:|A|+|B|=d\right\},
 \notag\\
 {\cal F}_{q_-}[d]
 &={\rm span}\left\{
 c_{-\lambda}J_{-\mu}v_-:|\lambda|+|\mu|=d\right\}.
 \label{eq:NS-D-and-F-spaces}
\end{align}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-triangular-L12}, a component of a vector in
\({\cal D}_{q_-}[d]\) can only have charge \(q_-+2p\), \(p\geq0\).
The operator \(L_0^{(1)}+L_0^{(2)}\) is the ambient free-field grading, so
every such component has relative energy \(d\).  The complex-fermion vacuum
energy is \(E_{\rm F}(q)=q^2/2\).  A component with
\(p\geq1\) therefore requires at least
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm F}(q_-+2p)-E_{\rm F}(q_-)
 =2pq_-+2p^2
 \geq 2q_-+2=d+1.
 \label{eq:NS-charge-raising-gap}
\end{equation}
Only level \(d\) is available, so every charge-raised component vanishes.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 {\cal D}_{q_-}[d]\subseteq {\cal F}_{q_-}[d].
 \label{eq:NS-D-subset-F}
\end{equation}

For generic \(b,P\), the Fermi-sea construction and character proof of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.2803}{arXiv:1111.2803}, Sec.~3.2.1, give
an injective tensor product of two Virasoro Verma modules generated by
\(v_-\).  If \(p(a)\) denotes the partition number, then
\begin{equation}
 \dim {\cal D}_{q_-}[d]
 =\sum_{a+b=d}p(a)p(b).
\end{equation}
Boson--fermion correspondence gives the explicit basis in the second line
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-D-and-F-spaces}, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \dim {\cal F}_{q_-}[d]
 =\sum_{a+b=d}p(a)p(b).
\end{equation}
These are concrete finite-dimensional subspaces of the same ambient Fock
space.  Thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-D-subset-F} and equality of dimensions imply
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{{\cal D}_{q_-}[d]={\cal F}_{q_-}[d].}
 \label{eq:NS-D-equals-F}
\end{equation}
In particular the current descendant in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-Heisenberg-construction} belongs to
\({\cal D}_{q_-}[d]\), proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-selection} for every
\(|n|\geq1\) at generic parameters.  This proves existence of a
double-Virasoro PBW expansion; it does not by itself give its coefficients.
At \(|n|=1\) the target is the common uncharged vacuum \(v_0\), so no
reflection convention is changed in this endpoint case.

\subsection{Relative current--PBW reconstruction in the human-note
conventions}
\label{sec:NS-relative-current-PBW}

We now turn the support statement into a finite algorithm for the
two-Virasoro PBW coefficients.  It is important not to obtain these
coefficients by first writing a Shapovalov projection
\(\langle L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}w_{n-1},L_1w_n\rangle\).
Although that formula is formally correct, evaluating its left-hand side
in this way requires either the unknown SCA-PBW expansion of \(w_n\) or an
explicit reflected free-field bra.  It therefore does not give an
independent constructive algorithm.

This subsection uses exactly the notation and normalization of the human
note.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \chi_r=\psi_r-i\eta_r,
 \qquad
 w_n(P)=
 \chi_{-(4n-1)/2}\chi_{-(4n-3)/2}\cdots\chi_{-1/2}|P\rangle,
 \label{eq:NS-human-raw-w}
\end{equation}
with no factor of \(1/\sqrt2\) in \(\chi\), while
\(\Psi=\chi/\sqrt2\) is used in the definition of the current \(J\).
An undecorated \(L_m\) is the physical SCA Virasoro mode.  For \(n\geq0\),
the normalized vector used elsewhere in this machine note and the raw
human-note vector are related by
\begin{equation}
 v_n=\mathfrak s_n(P)w_n,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak s_n(P)=2^{-2n}\ell(Q+2P,4n),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak s_0(P)=1.
 \label{eq:NS-human-v-w-scale}
\end{equation}

We first state the construction for \(n\geq1\).  Put
\begin{equation}
 w_-=w_{n-1},
 \qquad
 q=2n-2,
 \qquad
 d=4n-3=2q+1.
 \label{eq:NS-relative-data}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-Heisenberg-construction}, translated with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-human-v-w-scale}, becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 X_n:=L_1w_n
 =\kappa_n^{(w)}H_d^{(-2)}(J_-)w_-,
 \qquad
 \kappa_n^{(w)}
 =\kappa_n^{\mathrm{NS}}
   \frac{\mathfrak s_{n-1}(P)}{\mathfrak s_n(P)}.}
 \label{eq:NS-human-L1-current-state}
\end{equation}
The scalar \(\kappa_n^{(w)}\) depends on the raw-state ordering and the
pair-field cocycle; the displayed conversion fixes it from the conventions
already adopted above.  The PBW direction below is independent of this
overall scalar.

If \(\mu=(1^{m_1}2^{m_2}\cdots)\) is a partition, set
\begin{equation}
 J_{-\mu}=\prod_{r\geq1}J_{-r}^{m_r},
 \qquad
 z_\mu=\prod_{r\geq1}r^{m_r}m_r!,
 \qquad
 \ell(\mu)=\sum_{r\geq1}m_r.
\end{equation}
The Heisenberg polynomial in Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-human-L1-current-state} is
then
\begin{equation}
 H_d^{(-2)}(J_-)
 =\sum_{\mu\vdash d}
   \frac{(-2)^{\ell(\mu)}}{z_\mu}J_{-\mu}.
 \label{eq:NS-human-H-partition-expansion}
\end{equation}

The two relative level-\(d\) bases are
\begin{align}
 D_{A,B}
 &=L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}w_-,
 &|A|+|B|&=d,
 \label{eq:NS-relative-double-Vir-basis}\\
 F_{\lambda,\mu}
 &=c_{-\lambda}J_{-\mu}w_-,
 &|\lambda|+|\mu|&=d.
 \label{eq:NS-relative-current-basis}
\end{align}
Both have cardinality
\begin{equation}
 p_2(d)=\sum_{a=0}^dp(a)p(d-a).
\end{equation}
At generic \(b,P\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-D-equals-F} says that they are bases
of the same concrete subspace.

For positive-mode partitions define
\begin{equation}
 c_\lambda=\prod_{r\geq1}c_r^{m_r(\lambda)},
 \qquad
 J_\mu=\prod_{r\geq1}J_r^{m_r(\mu)}.
\end{equation}
Let \(\operatorname{Coeff}_{w_-}[Y]\) denote the coefficient of \(w_-\)
after a relative level-zero state \(Y\) has been fully reduced.  Define the
terminal-reduction matrix
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_{(\lambda,\mu),(A,B)}
 :=\operatorname{Coeff}_{w_-}\!\left[
 c_\lambda J_\mu
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}w_-
 \right],
 \qquad
 |\lambda|+|\mu|=|A|+|B|=d.}
 \label{eq:NS-terminal-reduction-matrix}
\end{equation}
This is an operator reduction, not a BPZ matrix element.  In particular,
it introduces neither a reflected bra nor the norm of \(w_-\).

The defining current properties of \(X_n\) give the complete right-hand
side.  Since \(c_mw_-=J_mw_-=0\) for \(m>0\), the Heisenberg relations and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-human-H-partition-expansion} imply
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 c_\lambda J_\mu X_n
 =\begin{cases}
   \kappa_n^{(w)}(-2)^{\ell(\mu)}w_-,
    &\lambda=\varnothing,\quad |\mu|=d,\\
   0,
    &\lambda\ne\varnothing,\quad |\lambda|+|\mu|=d.
  \end{cases}}
 \label{eq:NS-terminal-current-conditions}
\end{equation}
Consequently the desired human-note expansion
\begin{equation}
 L_1w_n
 =\sum_{|A|+|B|=d}C_{A,B}^{(n)}
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}w_{n-1}
 \label{eq:NS-human-double-Vir-expansion}
\end{equation}
is determined by the square system
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \sum_{|A|+|B|=d}
 K_{(\lambda,\mu),(A,B)}C_{A,B}^{(n)}
 =\delta_{\lambda,\varnothing}\,
  \kappa_n^{(w)}(-2)^{\ell(\mu)},
 \qquad |\lambda|+|\mu|=d.}
 \label{eq:NS-terminal-linear-system}
\end{equation}
Pure \(J\)-reductions alone give only \(p(d)\) equations and are not
sufficient once \(d>1\).  The vanishing equations with
\(\lambda\ne\varnothing\) supply the remaining conditions.

The entries of \(K\) are computed recursively in the free-field
realization of the embedded generators.  For example,
\begin{align}
 J_mD_{A,B}
 &=[J_m,L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}]w_-,
 \notag\\
 c_mD_{A,B}
 &=[c_m,L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}]w_-,
 \label{eq:NS-relative-positive-mode-reduction}
\end{align}
because the final positive mode annihilates \(w_-\).  Apply the Leibniz
rule to the mode word, substitute Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-two-Vir-U-explicit}, and
normal order after every step relative to the charged vacuum \(w_-\).  No
expansion of either \(w_-\) or \(w_n\) over the original SCA vacuum is
needed.

The charge gap makes this reduction close on the basis
\eqref{eq:NS-relative-current-basis}.  Indeed,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-triangular-L12} writes each embedded mode as a
charge-preserving term plus a term that raises the outward current charge
by two.  Starting at charge \(q\), the latter would require relative energy
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm F}(q+2)-E_{\rm F}(q)=2q+2=d+1,
 \end{equation}
whereas every intermediate state in the level-\(d\) reduction has at most
energy \(d\).  All charge-raised terms therefore vanish identically, not
merely after the final projection.

For completeness, one may instead construct the relative change-of-basis
matrix \(T\) defined by
\begin{equation}
 D_{A,B}
 =\sum_{|\lambda|+|\mu|=d}
 T_{(\lambda,\mu),(A,B)}F_{\lambda,\mu}.
 \label{eq:NS-relative-transition-matrix}
\end{equation}
Ordinary Heisenberg reduction gives
\begin{equation}
 K_{(\lambda,\mu),(A,B)}
 =z_\lambda z_\mu
  T_{(\lambda,\mu),(A,B)}.
 \label{eq:NS-K-versus-T}
\end{equation}
Thus invertibility of \(K\) follows from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-D-equals-F}, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-terminal-linear-system}
is equivalent to coefficient matching against the known vector
\begin{equation}
 x_{\lambda,\mu}
 =\delta_{\lambda,\varnothing}\,
  \kappa_n^{(w)}\frac{(-2)^{\ell(\mu)}}{z_\mu}.
\end{equation}
At a Kac-degenerate value one performs the construction at generic \(b,P\)
and then takes the limit, or works directly in the irreducible quotient.

Applying physical \(L_1\) to the explicit level-two vector recorded earlier

 L_1v_\sigma

 \left(bL_{-1}^{(1)}+b^{-1}L_{-1}^{(2)}\right)v_0

This agrees with Eq.~\eqref{eq:NS-L1-Heisenberg-construction}, since

\section{Ramond extension: status of the literature}
\label{sec:ramond-literature-status}

This section records the literature status as of 20 August 2026.  It is
important to distinguish three increasingly strong statements:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the decomposition of an \(\Ff_0\oplus\SCA_0\) Ramond module into
 two-Virasoro modules;
 \item a norm or Whittaker overlap of one two-Virasoro highest vector; and
 \item the generic, chiral \((\mathrm{NS},\mathrm R,\mathrm R)\) three-point
 coefficient, including its Ramond-ground-state phases.
\end{enumerate}
Only the third statement supplies the coefficient required by the human
note.  In its notation the target is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb B_f^{(\eta)}
 (h_1,\beta_2,\beta_3;n_1,n_2,n_3)
 =
 \frac{
  \widehat\rho_f^{(\eta)}
  (v_{1,n_1},v^{\alpha_2}_{2,n_2},v^{\alpha_3}_{3,n_3})
 }{
  \lVert v_{1,n_1}\rVert
  \lVert v^{\alpha_2}_{2,n_2}\rVert
  \lVert v^{\alpha_3}_{3,n_3}\rVert
 },
 \label{eq:ramond-target-B}
\end{equation}
where \(n_1\in\frac12\mathbb Z\),
\(n_2,n_3\in\frac12\mathbb Z+\frac14\), and the parity labels and the
chiral sign \(\eta\) must be retained.

\subsection{What has actually been established}

\paragraph{Gauge-theory and block evidence.}
Ito's ALE-space proposal, arXiv:1110.2176, identifies Ramond Whittaker norms
and overlaps with instanton partition functions.  Belavin--Mukhametzhanov,
arXiv:1210.7454, similarly obtain four-point blocks containing Ramond fields
from AGT data.  These works provide strong independent control of Ramond
blocks, but neither computes Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-target-B}: their algebra and
basis are not the present \(\Vir\oplus\Vir\subset\Ff\oplus\SCA\) branching
basis, and they do not determine its chiral three-point normalization.

\paragraph{The all-label double-Liouville candidate.}
Schomerus--Suchanek, arXiv:1210.1856, construct an infinite tower
\(\Phi^{(k)}_\alpha\) in super-Liouville theory times a free fermion.  Integer
\(k\) belongs to the NS sector and half-integer \(k\) to the Ramond sector.
They propose that every \(\Phi^{(k)}_\alpha\) is proportional to a primary of
the two bosonic Liouville theories; this is precisely the local-field version
of the branching-primary identification needed here.  They give a candidate
normalization and test several nontrivial local three-point functions,
including Ramond labels \(k=\pm\frac12\) and \(\pm\frac32\).

This is the closest result in the literature to a formula for the full
Ramond branching coefficient, but it is not yet Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-target-B}.
The paper explicitly does not prove the three-point identity for arbitrary
labels.  Its checks concern local, nonchiral correlators and leave a fourth
root of unity \(\varpi(k_3,k_2,k_1)\), with \(\varpi^4=1\).  Consequently it
does not fix the separate holomorphic coefficient, the two Ramond parity
copies, or the phases associated with a convention such as
\begin{equation}
 G_0w_\beta^\pm=i\beta e^{\mp i\pi/4}w_\beta^\mp.
 \label{eq:ramond-G0-phase-status}
\end{equation}
It should be used below as an all-label candidate and a magnitude check, not
as an already proved chiral branching formula.

\paragraph{The screening-charge proof stops in the NS sector.}
Hadasz--Jask\'olski, arXiv:1312.4520, formulate the correspondence in terms
of chiral structure constants and give a complete proof in the NS sector.
Their reflection map, screening-charge matrix elements, singular-vector
residues, and factorized chiral constants are the direct template for the
calculation proposed below.  They cite the Ramond tests of
Schomerus--Suchanek and remark that a Ramond extension appears
straightforward, but they do not carry out that extension.  In particular,
they do not introduce the Ramond zero-mode Clifford module needed to resolve
the two parity copies in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-target-B}.

\paragraph{The Ramond module and highest vectors are known.}
Bershtein--Shchechkin, arXiv:1608.02568, prove the generic Ramond branching
rule
\begin{equation}
 \M_{\mathrm R}(\beta)
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{\epsilon=0,1}
 \bigoplus_{n\in\frac12\mathbb Z+\frac14}
 \V_{c^{(1)}}(h_n^{(1)})\otimes
 \V_{c^{(2)}}(h_n^{(2)}),
 \label{eq:ramond-literature-branching}
\end{equation}
and give explicit highest vectors as finite products of integer modes
\(\chi_r^\pm=\psi_r-i\eta_r^\pm\), including the zero-mode factors that
distinguish the two parity copies.  Thus the vectorial input for a direct
free-field calculation is available.

The same paper does \emph{not} obtain generic three-point matrix elements.
For the much weaker irregular/Whittaker overlap it states a conjectural
squared coefficient in terms of the odd product \(s_{\mathrm{odd}}\).  The
conjecture was checked by computer for \(\lvert n\rvert\leq9/4\) (and in the
reported series test through relative order ten), while its sign remains
undetermined.  This supplies a valuable norm/irregular limit check, but not
the coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-target-B}.

\section{Constructive physical-\texorpdfstring{\(L_1\)}{L1} action:
Ramond sector}
\label{sec:constructive-physical-L1-R}

This section gives the corresponding construction for the Ramond branching
primaries.  Here all fermionic modes are integers,
\(\delta_b=b^{-1}-b\), and
\(U_m=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}\psi_{m-r}G_r\).  An undecorated \(L_m\) denotes the
\emph{physical} SCA mode, not either embedded Virasoro generator.

For \(m\ne0\), the complete embedded generators are
\begin{align}
 L_m^{(1)}
 &=\frac{b^{-1}}{\delta_b}L_m
   -\frac{b^{-1}+2b}{\delta_b}L_m^{\Ff}
   +\frac1{\delta_b}U_m,
 \notag\\
 L_m^{(2)}
 &=-\frac b{\delta_b}L_m
   +\frac{b+2b^{-1}}{\delta_b}L_m^{\Ff}
   -\frac1{\delta_b}U_m.
 \label{eq:R-two-Vir-U-explicit}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{align}
 L_m^{(1)}+L_m^{(2)}&=L_m+L_m^{\Ff},
 \label{eq:R-two-Vir-sum}\\
 bL_m^{(1)}+b^{-1}L_m^{(2)}&=2Q L_m^{\Ff}-U_m.
 \label{eq:R-two-Vir-weighted-sum}
\end{align}
The \(\psi G\) term is present in each generator.  It cancels only in the first
combination and remains explicitly in the second.  No \(\psi G\) term is omitted
in what follows.  The restriction to \(m\ne0\) avoids the Ramond
normal-ordering constant, which is irrelevant for the \(L_{\pm1}\) argument.

If \(W_n^\epsilon\) is a
\(\Vir^{(1)}\oplus\Vir^{(2)}\) highest vector, then
\begin{equation}
 L_m^{(1)}W_n^\epsilon=L_m^{(2)}W_n^\epsilon=0,
 \qquad m>0.
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:R-two-Vir-sum}, with its canceled \(U_m\) terms, then
gives
\begin{equation}
 L_mW_n^\epsilon=-L_m^{\Ff}W_n^\epsilon.
 \label{eq:physical-L-on-branch-primary}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Selection rule and exact low-level reductions}

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\(L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}W_{n'}^\epsilon\) satisfying
\(N_{n'}+|A|+|B|=N_n-1\).  Direct free-field reduction instead gives the

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1W_n^\epsilon\ \in\
 \operatorname{span}\left\{
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}W_{n-\operatorname{sgn}(n)}^\epsilon:
 |A|+|B|=4|n|-3

 \label{eq:ramond-L1-selection}

\subsection{Constructive free-field proof of the current-descendant formula}

We now isolate which part of the preceding observation can be proved for
all labels.  All fermionic modes in this argument are Ramond integer modes.
Choose the reflected free-field realization adapted to the sign of the
branch label, suppress that sign from the notation, and introduce the second
isotropic fermion
\begin{equation}
 \chi_r=\psi_r-i\eta_r,
 \qquad
 \bar\chi_r=\psi_r+i\eta_r,
 \qquad
 \Psi_r=\frac{\chi_r}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \Psi_r^*=\frac{\bar\chi_r}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:ramond-complex-fermion}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \{\Psi_r,\Psi_s\}=\{\Psi_r^*,\Psi_s^*\}=0,
 \qquad
 \{\Psi_r,\Psi_s^*\}=\delta_{r+s,0}.
\end{equation}
Put \(\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(n)\).  As in the NS construction, orient
the current so that the finite-sea fermion has charge \(\sigma\):
\begin{equation}
 [J_m,J_k]=m\delta_{m+k,0},
 \qquad
 [J_0,\Psi_r]=\sigma\Psi_r,
 \qquad
 [J_0,\Psi_r^*]=-\sigma\Psi_r^*.
 \label{eq:ramond-current-convention}
\end{equation}
For \(n>0\), this current is
\(J(z)=:\!\Psi(z)\Psi^*(z)\!:\); for \(n<0\), the negative of the
reflected current is used.

There is an important convention at a sign-crossing transition.  For each
fixed transition \(n\to n-\sigma\), the free-field realization and the
orientation of \(J\) are chosen from the source label \(n\) and are then
held fixed on both the source and target states.  In particular, for
\(n=3/4\) the target \(W_{-1/4}^\epsilon\) is represented in the same
current orientation as \(W_{3/4}^\epsilon\).  The reflected realization
used to write a negative-label vector as a source of a different
transition must not be introduced halfway through the argument: charges
defined with two oppositely oriented currents cannot be compared.

The finite products in Eqs.~(4.24)--(4.26) of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02568}{arXiv:1608.02568} say precisely that,
in either parity copy, the branching vector is a charged complex-fermion
vacuum:
\begin{equation}
 W_n^\epsilon={\cal N}_n^\epsilon
 |q=2n;\epsilon,P\rangle,
 \qquad
 J_m|q;\epsilon,P\rangle=0\quad(m>0).
 \label{eq:ramond-W-as-charged-vacuum}
\end{equation}
The two values of \(\epsilon\) are two independent copies of the same
charged Fock construction.  The auxiliary and physical Ramond zero modes
fix the normalization factors \({\cal N}_n^\epsilon\); they do not change the
charge \(q=2n\).

Let \(L_m^\eta\) denote the physical-fermion part of \(L_m\).  Since
\(W_n^\epsilon\) contains no physical-boson oscillator, the positive
physical mode satisfies
\begin{equation}
 L_1W_n^\epsilon=L_1^\eta W_n^\epsilon.
\end{equation}
On the other hand, Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-L-on-branch-primary} gives
\((L_1^{\Ff}+L_1^\eta)W_n^\epsilon=0\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 L_1W_n^\epsilon
 =-\frac12(L_1^{\Ff}-L_1^\eta)W_n^\epsilon.
 \label{eq:ramond-L1-stress-difference}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-complex-fermion} into the two
Majorana stress tensors gives the operator identity
\begin{equation}
 L_m^{\Ff}-L_m^\eta
 =\frac14\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}r\left(
 :\!\chi_{m-r}\chi_r\!:
 +:\!\bar\chi_{m-r}\bar\chi_r\!:
 \right).
 \label{eq:ramond-stress-difference-pairs}
\end{equation}
Thus the physical \(L_1\) is reduced, on a branching highest vector, to a
sum of two charge-changing fermion-pair modes.

This identity proves the triangular selection among charged free-fermion
vacua.  A charge
\(q\) complex-fermion vacuum has diagonal fermion energy \(q^2/2\).
After applying a mode of index one, the energy is \(q^2/2-1\).  The
\(\chi\chi\) term has charge \(+2\sigma\), so it would increase \(|q|\) by
two.  Its lowest possible energy is too large, and it annihilates the
state.  The conjugate \(\bar\chi\bar\chi\) pair has charge
\(-2\sigma\) and sends
\(q\) to \(q-2\sigma=2(n-\operatorname{sgn}(n))\).  The excitation level
over the new charged vacuum is
\begin{equation}
 d=\frac{q^2}{2}-1-\frac{(|q|-2)^2}{2}
  =2|q|-3=4|n|-3.
 \label{eq:ramond-pair-descendant-level}
\end{equation}
For \(|n|=1/4\), this number is negative and the image vanishes.  The pair
operator is even, so it cannot mix the two parity copies.

The same argument gives an explicit current descendant rather than only its
charge.  Its conversion into the double-Virasoro descendant space is proved
by the triangular filtration below.
Define the opposite-charge spin-two field
\begin{equation}
 {\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z)
 =:\!\Psi^*(z)\partial\Psi^*(z)\!:
 =\sum_m{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,m}z^{-m-2}.
\end{equation}
It has current charge \(-2\sigma\), and antisymmetry gives
\begin{equation}
 {\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,m}
 =-\sum_r r:\!\Psi^*_{m-r}\Psi^*_r\!:.
\end{equation}
Combining this with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ramond-L1-stress-difference} and
\eqref{eq:ramond-stress-difference-pairs} yields the exact branchwise
operator formula
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1W_n^\epsilon
 =\frac14{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,1}W_n^\epsilon,
 \qquad \sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(n).}
 \label{eq:ramond-L1-pair-field}
\end{equation}

We next derive the conversion of the pair state into a current descendant
directly from free-fermion commutators.  This step is more than the
particle--hole description and does not assume a bosonized vertex formula.
Put
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=-2\sigma,
 \qquad q=2n.
 \label{eq:ramond-pair-alpha-q}
\end{equation}
The current convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-current-convention} gives
\begin{equation}
 [J_m,\Psi^*(z)]=-\sigma z^m\Psi^*(z).
\end{equation}
When the commutator is differentiated, one apparently obtains an extra
term proportional to
\(m z^{m-1}:\!\Psi^*(z)\Psi^*(z)\!:\).  This term vanishes by fermionic
antisymmetry.  The direct free-fermion commutator is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 [J_m,{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z)]
 =\alpha z^m{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z).}
 \label{eq:ramond-current-pair-commutator}
\end{equation}

Let
\begin{equation}
 F_q(z):={\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z)
 |q;\epsilon,P\rangle.
\end{equation}
For every \(m>0\), Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ramond-W-as-charged-vacuum} and
\eqref{eq:ramond-current-pair-commutator} imply
\begin{equation}
 J_mF_q(z)=\alpha z^mF_q(z).
 \label{eq:ramond-current-eigenvector-equations}
\end{equation}
On the other hand,
\begin{equation}
 \left[J_m,
 \alpha\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{J_{-k}}{k}z^k\right]
 =\alpha z^m.
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}
 \exp\left(-\alpha\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{J_{-k}}{k}z^k\right)
 F_q(z)
\end{equation}
is killed by every positive current mode.  It has charge \(q+\alpha\),
fixed physical momentum \(P\), and the same Ramond copy \(\epsilon\).  The
Heisenberg Fock module has a unique highest vector with these data, hence
\begin{equation}
 F_q(z)=\nu_\alpha z^s
 \exp\left(\alpha\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{J_{-m}}{m}z^m\right)
 |q+\alpha;\epsilon,P\rangle
 \label{eq:ramond-pair-current-solution}
\end{equation}
for a convention-dependent constant \(\nu_\alpha\).

The power \(s\) is fixed without a vertex-operator assumption.  The pair
field has weight two, while a charge-\(q\) complex-fermion vacuum has weight
\(q^2/2\), up to the common Ramond normal-ordering constant.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 s=\frac{(q+\alpha)^2}{2}-\frac{q^2}{2}-2
 =\alpha q,
 \qquad \alpha^2=4.
 \label{eq:ramond-pair-current-power}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:ramond-pair-current-solution} and
\eqref{eq:ramond-pair-current-power} are the usual charge-two
boson--fermion formula, but here they have been obtained directly by solving
the current commutator equations.

Introduce the universal polynomials
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{d\geq0}H_d^{(\alpha)}(J_-)z^d
 =\exp\left(\alpha\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{J_{-m}}{m}z^m\right),
 \qquad
 dH_d^{(\alpha)}
 =\alpha\sum_{m=1}^dJ_{-m}H_{d-m}^{(\alpha)}.
 \label{eq:ramond-Heisenberg-descendants}
\end{equation}
Since
\({\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_\sigma(z)=\sum_m
{\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,m}z^{-m-2}\), the mode
\({\cal P}^{\mathrm{opp}}_{\sigma,1}\) is the coefficient of \(z^{-3}\).
The term \(H_d^{(\alpha)}\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-pair-current-solution} has power
\(z^{\alpha q+d}\).  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \alpha q+d=-3,
 \qquad
 d=-3-\alpha q=4|n|-3.
 \label{eq:ramond-pair-mode-selection}
\end{equation}
This gives the exact current-descendant formula
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_1W_n^\epsilon
 =\kappa_n^\epsilon
 H_{4|n|-3}^{(-2\operatorname{sgn}(n))}(J_-)
 W_{n-\operatorname{sgn}(n)}^\epsilon,
 \qquad |n|\geq\frac34.}
 \label{eq:ramond-L1-Heisenberg-construction}
\end{equation}
Using the constant \(\nu_{-2\sigma}\) defined directly by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-pair-current-solution}, together with the normalization
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-W-as-charged-vacuum}, gives
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_n^\epsilon
 =\frac{\nu_{-2\operatorname{sgn}(n)}}4
  \frac{{\cal N}_n^\epsilon}
       {{\cal N}_{n-\operatorname{sgn}(n)}^\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:ramond-kappa-normalization}
\end{equation}
Consequently the remaining overall factor is a finite-sea and zero-mode
normalization, not an undetermined dynamical coefficient.

For the positive branch the first three polynomials needed here are
\begin{align}
 H_0^{(-2)}={}&1,
 &
 H_2^{(-2)}={}&2J_{-1}^2-J_{-2},
 \notag\\
 H_4^{(-2)}={}&\frac23J_{-1}^4-2J_{-1}^2J_{-2}
 +\frac12J_{-2}^2+\frac43J_{-1}J_{-3}-\frac12J_{-4}.
 \label{eq:ramond-first-Heisenberg-polynomials}
\end{align}
They correspond respectively to \(n=3/4,5/4,7/4\).  In particular,
\(H_0=1\) explains why the first image is itself a lower branch primary,
whereas \(H_2\) and \(H_4\) give the first genuine descendants.

\subsection{Triangular \texorpdfstring{\(J_0\)}{J0} filtration and support
theorem}

The NS decomposition
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:NS-triangular-K}--\eqref{eq:NS-triangular-L12} uses only
the free-field formulas at nonzero mode number.  It therefore carries over
to Ramond integer modes.  In the reflected realization selected by the
source label, put \(\widehat J_0=\sigma J_0\).  Then, for every \(m\ne0\),
\begin{align}
 L_m^{(1)}&=\ell_m^{(1)}-\frac{Q}{2\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)},
 &
 L_m^{(2)}&=\ell_m^{(2)}+\frac{Q}{2\delta_b}X_m^{(+2)},
 \notag\\
 [\widehat J_0,\ell_m^{(i)}]&=0,
 &
 [\widehat J_0,X_m^{(+2)}]&=2X_m^{(+2)},
 \label{eq:R-triangular-L12}
\end{align}
where now
\begin{equation}
 X_m^{(+2)}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}r:\!\chi_{m-r}\chi_r\!:.
\end{equation}
The Ramond normal-ordering constant affects only mode zero and hence does
not enter the descendant argument.

For \(|n|\geq5/4\), source and adjacent target use the same reflected
realization.  Set
\begin{equation}
 W_-^\epsilon=W_{n-\sigma}^\epsilon,
 \qquad
 q_-=2|n|-2,
 \qquad
 d=4|n|-3=2q_-+1.
\end{equation}
Within a fixed parity copy define
\begin{align}
 {\cal D}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d]
 &={\rm span}\left\{
 L_{-A}^{(1)}L_{-B}^{(2)}W_-^\epsilon:
 |A|+|B|=d\right\},
 \notag\\
 {\cal F}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d]
 &={\rm span}\left\{
 c_{-\lambda}J_{-\mu}W_-^\epsilon:
 |\lambda|+|\mu|=d\right\}.
 \label{eq:R-D-and-F-spaces}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:R-triangular-L12} allows only charges
\(q_-+2p\), \(p\geq0\), in the first space.  The common Ramond vacuum
constant cancels from energy differences, and
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm F}(q_-+2p)-E_{\rm F}(q_-)
 =2pq_-+2p^2
 \geq 2q_-+2=d+1
 \qquad(p\geq1).
 \label{eq:R-charge-raising-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus no charge-raised component fits at level \(d\), and
\begin{equation}
 {\cal D}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d]
 \subseteq {\cal F}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d].
\end{equation}

Theorem~4.2 of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02568}{arXiv:1608.02568} proves that, for
generic momentum, each \(W_-^\epsilon\) generates an irreducible and
linearly independent tensor product of two Virasoro Verma modules.  With
the same partition-number notation as above, this gives
\begin{equation}
 \dim {\cal D}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d]
 =\sum_{a+b=d}p(a)p(b)
 =\dim {\cal F}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d].
\end{equation}
The second equality follows from the two independent oscillator sets
\(c_{-m}\) and \(J_{-m}\); fixing \(\epsilon\) removes the Ramond ground-space
multiplicity.  The preceding inclusion is therefore an equality:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 {\cal D}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d]
 ={\cal F}_{q_-,\epsilon}[d].}
 \label{eq:R-D-equals-F}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ramond-L1-Heisenberg-construction} then proves the
all-label support rule \eqref{eq:ramond-L1-selection} for
\(|n|\geq5/4\).

The sign-crossing case \(|n|=3/4\) is a separate zero-mode base case:
\(d=0\), and Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-L1-three-quarter} identifies the image
directly with the opposite \(\mp1/4\) primary in the same parity copy.
Together with \(L_1W_{\pm1/4}^\epsilon=0\), this proves the Ramond support
rule for every branch label at generic parameters.  No assertion that
\(J_0\) is constant on a double-Virasoro module is used.

\paragraph{What remains constructive.}
The argument proves existence and uniqueness of the PBW expansion but does
not give its coefficients.  At level \(d\), a direct algorithm is now
well-defined:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item expand the \(p_2(d)=\sum_{a+b=d}p(a)p(b)\) ordered
 double-Virasoro PBW descendants in the fixed-charge basis
 \(c_{-\lambda}J_{-\mu}W_-^\epsilon\);
 \item invert this square generic matrix on the coefficient vector of
 \(H_d^{(-2\sigma)}(J_-)W_-^\epsilon\);
 \item multiply by the normalization \(\kappa_n^\epsilon\) from
 Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-kappa-normalization}.
\end{enumerate}
Equation~\eqref{eq:R-D-equals-F} proves that the matrix in the first step is
invertible at generic parameters.  A factorization into two Virasoro chain
vectors could simplify this inversion, but is not needed for the support
theorem.  The remaining normalization problem is to evaluate the cocycle
and Ramond zero-mode ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-kappa-normalization} for
both parity copies.

\section{Ramond literature verdict and remaining problem}

\paragraph{The recent screening algebra does not close the gap.}
Ohkubo, arXiv:2505.02926, constructs a \(q\)-deformation of
\(\Ff\oplus\SCA\), two commuting \(q\)-Virasoro algebras, and screening
currents whose degeneration gives the super-Virasoro screenings.  The
representation theory in that paper is NS.  Its conclusion says that a
modified Fock module and half-integer highest-weight charge should allow the
Ramond sector; no Ramond branching coefficient is computed.

\subsection{Verdict}

No source found in the literature through the date above derives the generic
chiral coefficient \(\mathbb B_f^{(\eta)}\) for all branching labels while
also fixing the two Ramond ground-state copies and their phases.  The present
status is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{Ramond branching rule and explicit highest vectors: proved,}\\
 \text{all-label local double-Liouville formula: proposed and tested,}\\
 \text{generic chiral branching coefficient with phases: open.}
 \end{gathered}}
 \label{eq:ramond-literature-verdict}
\end{equation}
This conclusion is narrower than saying that Ramond blocks or Ramond
super-Liouville three-point functions are unknown: both are known.  What is
missing is their convention-complete projection onto the specific
two-Virasoro branching highest vectors.

\subsection{Screening-charge route to be pursued here}

The most economical derivation is now well localized:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Use the integer-mode products of arXiv:1608.02568 as the definitions of
 the two Ramond branching vectors \(v_n^\epsilon(P)\), including their
 \(\chi_0^\pm\) factors.
 \item Extend the reflected-Fock-space BPZ pairing used in the NS calculation
 to the four-dimensional zero-mode ground space
 \(\Ff_0\otimes\SCA_0\).  The pairing must implement
 Eq.~\eqref{eq:ramond-G0-phase-status}, rather than identify the two parity
 copies implicitly.
 \item Generalize the screening-charge matrix elements of
 arXiv:1312.4520 from NS exponentials to the spin-field intertwiners of an
 \((\mathrm{NS},\mathrm R,\mathrm R)\) vertex.  Charge neutrality fixes the
 number of screenings; the zero-mode Clifford matrix fixes which of the two
 chiral Ramond structures is selected.
 \item Evaluate the resulting finite Selberg-type products and divide by the
 three reflected-Fock norms.  The product must reduce to the
 Schomerus--Suchanek double-Liouville candidate after multiplying the two
 chiral halves and forgetting the zero-mode phase.
 \item Anchor the remaining square-root choices at
 \(n_2,n_3=\pm\frac14\) by a direct ground-state Ward-identity calculation,
 then propagate them by the screening shifts.  In the Whittaker limit the
 square must reproduce the \(s_{\mathrm{odd}}\) formula conjectured and tested
 in arXiv:1608.02568.
\end{enumerate}
The first new calculation should therefore be the four-by-four matrix of
ground-level \((\mathrm{NS},\mathrm R,\mathrm R)\) branching numerators.  It
will separate a genuine dynamical product formula from the convention-only
zero-mode phases before higher integer \(\chi_{-r}\) modes are introduced.

\section{What remains to be audited}

The calculation above settles the all-NS coefficient, modulo a direct audit
of the literature product formula against the human note's ordered Ward
identities.  The efficient audit is:

 \item reproduce Eqs.~\eqref{eq:one-branch-l} and
 Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-branch-l} from the three-point Ward identities;

 \item only then use Eq.~\eqref{eq:B-final} as the all-level input to the

\end{enumerate}

The Ramond branching vectors have an analogous free-field prescription in

 G_0w_\beta^\pm=i\beta e^{\mp i\pi/4}w_\beta^\mp.
