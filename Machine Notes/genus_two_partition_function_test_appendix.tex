\section{Super-Liouville partition functions in the theta channel}
\label{app:partition-test}

We give the three-point coefficients and sewing formulas used in
the cross-channel test, in the notation of the paper:
$\mathbf F^{(a)}(h_{1,2,3};q_{1,2,3},\eta_{1,2,3})$ for all-NS
and $\mathbf F^{(f,\eta,\eta')}(h_1,\beta_{2,3};q_{1,2,3},\eta_{1,2,3})$
for NS--R--R. The slots are $(\infty,1,0)$, so edge 1 is NS in
the latter channel. The signs $\eta,\eta'$ label the two
three-point forms, while $\eta_e$ label the tubes.

The blocks of the main notes retain the algebraic pairings and ordered
vertices specified in Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}. A diagonal physical
partition decomposition uses the geometrically sewn blocks, obtained by
converting the inverse Grams and infinity-slot vertices. We derive the
conversion first. The literal blocks of the main notes do not give a
fixed-spin partition contribution when squared diagonally. The algebraic
block algorithms are unchanged; the geometric phase is applied to each
coefficient in both channels before fixed-spin evaluation.

The spectrum is parameterized by real $p_e>0$, with $P_e=ip_e$
in the chiral block convention and measure $dp_e/\pi$ on each edge:
\begin{equation}
 Q=b+b^{-1},\qquad c=\frac32+3Q^2,\qquad
 h_{\NS}(p)=\frac{Q^2}{8}+\frac{p^2}{2},\qquad
 h_{\R}(p)=h_{\NS}(p)+\frac1{16},\qquad
 \beta_e=\frac{ip_e}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:partition-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The continuum two-point coefficient is $\pi\delta(p-p')$, both
for an NS primary and for each physical Ramond family.
The blocks omit primary powers, so every partition
function below includes $\prod_{e=1}^3|q_e^{h_e}|^2$, with
$q_e^{h_e}=\exp(h_e\operatorname{Log}q_e)$ in the chosen plumbing
frame. There is no additional $q_e^{-c/24}$ in this
plane-coordinate sewing prescription.

\subsection{Algebraic pairings and geometric BPZ sewing}
\label{app:partition-bpz}

The adjoint in \eqref{eq:bpz} is a linear, ordinary order-reversing
anti-involution: $(XY)^\dagger=Y^\dagger X^\dagger$, with
$G_r^\dagger=G_{-r}$ and $\psi_r^\dagger=-\psi_{-r}$.
It defines the stored contravariant Grams. Thus
$\langle\!\langle G_{-1/2}\phi|G_{-1/2}\phi\rangle=2h$ and
$\mathbb G_\beta|_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$.
The term ``BPZ'' used for these algebraic pairings earlier in the notes
must be distinguished from the geometric pairing below.

The distinction comes from the spin lift of inversion. For $w=q/z$,
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{\frac{dw}{dz}}=\pm\frac{i\sqrt q}{z}.
 \label{eq:partition-spin-inversion}
\end{equation}
The sign specifies the lift; the factor $i$ is fixed by the derivative.
Choose the branch in which geometric adjointness is
\begin{gather}
 L_n^{\ddagger}=L_{-n},\qquad
 G_r^{\ddagger}=iG_{-r},\qquad
 \psi_r^{\ddagger}=-i\psi_{-r},\nonumber\\
 (XY)^{\ddagger}=(-1)^{\epsilon_X\epsilon_Y}
                  Y^{\ddagger}X^{\ddagger},\nonumber\\
 \langle\!\langle G_r x|y\rangle_{\mathrm{geom}}
 =(-1)^{\epsilon_x}
   \langle\!\langle x|iG_{-r}y\rangle_{\mathrm{geom}}.
 \label{eq:partition-geometric-adjoint}
\end{gather}
Here $\epsilon_X,\epsilon_Y$ are operator parities and $\epsilon_x$
is the parity of the homogeneous state $x$. The adjoint is graded and
its square acts as parity on odd modes. It is not the ordinary
anti-involution used in the numerical Ward solver. These rules are
the Li--Zamolodchikov pairing of
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.5203}{Tuite--Zuevsky,
Eqs.~(10), (23), and (26)}, specialized to the supercurrent and free
fermion. The opposite lift changes the reference tube signs consistently.

With the even ground normalization fixed, the conversion is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \langle\!\langle x|y\rangle_{\mathrm{geom}}
     =i^{\epsilon_x}\langle\!\langle x|y\rangle,
 \qquad \epsilon_x=\epsilon_y.}
 \label{eq:partition-bpz-conversion}
\end{equation}
The exponent is parity represented by 0 or 1, not descendant level.
To verify the formula on an odd generator, use
$i^{(\epsilon_x+1)\bmod2}=(-1)^{\epsilon_x}i\,i^{\epsilon_x}$
and the ordinary contravariance of the stored Gram. Even generators
preserve parity, so their adjointness is unchanged. This proves the
conversion at every descendant level. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \langle\!\langle G_{-1/2}\phi|G_{-1/2}\phi\rangle_{\mathrm{geom}}
 =2ih,\qquad
 \mathbb G_{\beta,\mathrm{geom}}\big|_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
 \label{eq:partition-geometric-ground-metrics}
\end{equation}
The Ramond result also follows directly from the stated $G_0w^\pm$
and \eqref{eq:partition-geometric-adjoint}. It does not require a
change of the $G_0$ matrix elements. In the conjugate antiholomorphic
branch the conversion is $(-i)^{\widetilde\epsilon_x}$.

The vertex must be converted at the same time. The ordered matrix
element uses its first state as the infinity-slot bra, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\mathrm{geom}}(x_1,x_2,x_3)
 =i^{\epsilon_1}\rho(x_1,x_2,x_3).
 \label{eq:partition-geometric-vertex}
\end{equation}
This applies to either physical three-point form; their prescribed
ground values have an even first state and are unchanged. For a closed
ordered plumbing graph, each summand of the stored physical block
therefore acquires
\begin{equation}
 \prod_e(-i)^{\epsilon_e}\prod_v i^{\epsilon_{v,1}}.
 \label{eq:partition-local-bpz-factors}
\end{equation}
The first product comes from inverse Grams and the second from vertices.
The original $(-1)^{K_\Gamma}$ is retained during this conversion.
Neither a replacement of the Gram alone nor a deletion of $K_\Gamma$
alone specifies geometric sewing on an arbitrary graph. In the
implementation the algebraic coefficients are recovered first, and
the local conversion is applied to each target coefficient before
the block is evaluated.

For theta the cancellation is particularly simple. At an all-NS vertex
$\sum_e\epsilon_e=a\pmod2$, and
\begin{equation}
 K=\epsilon_1\epsilon_2+\epsilon_1\epsilon_3+\epsilon_2\epsilon_3,
 \qquad
 (-1)^K(-i)^{\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+\epsilon_3}=(-i)^a.
 \label{eq:partition-theta-bpz-cancellation}
\end{equation}
The two infinity-slot vertices additionally give
$(-1)^{\epsilon_1}$, which reverses the reference lift on edge 1.
Thus the geometric contraction, written using the stored tensors and
Grams, has only a constant $(-i)^a$ and this reference-lift change;
there is no remaining quadratic $K$. The constant cancels against its
antiholomorphic counterpart. The same chiral identity holds with $a$
replaced by $f$ in NS--R--R.

One can check the full all-NS state sum before holomorphic
factorization. Its graded product metric contributes
$(-1)^{\sum_e\epsilon_e\widetilde\epsilon_e}$, and
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 &(-1)^{K(\epsilon+\widetilde\epsilon)
                      +\sum_e\epsilon_e\widetilde\epsilon_e}
  (-i)^{\sum_e\epsilon_e}i^{\sum_e\widetilde\epsilon_e}
  =(-1)^a,\\
 &\hspace{12mm}
 \sum_e\epsilon_e\equiv\sum_e\widetilde\epsilon_e\equiv a\pmod2.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:partition-full-bpz-sign}
\end{equation}
The infinity factors have been absorbed into the reference lifts in
this equation. The remaining $(-1)^a$ multiplying $C_a^2$ must still
be kept. Equation~\eqref{eq:correlator-sign} is a valid algebraic
parity identity, but the physical interpretation of
\eqref{eq:correlator} requires these local BPZ factors. Factoring an
unconverted starting state sum cannot supply them afterwards.

The first discrepancy is already visible for a physical free Majorana,
without Liouville structure constants. With unit Fock norms, the three
two-fermion sphere matrix elements are one. The literal $K$-weighted
sewing at $(\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3)=(1,1,1)$ begins
\begin{equation}
 1-\sqrt{q_1q_2}-\sqrt{q_1q_3}-\sqrt{q_2q_3}
   +\text{terms of total level at least 2}.
 \label{eq:partition-first-mismatch}
\end{equation}
The geometric spin with positive charge-lattice character has all
three plus signs. The BPZ conversion above gives them directly.
Rescaling a structure constant cannot change these relative signs,
and relabeling the lifts cannot do so either: it would require
$\eta_1\eta_2=\eta_1\eta_3=\eta_2\eta_3=-1$, whose product is
impossible. The discrepancy is consequently a local sewing-convention
mismatch, not a failure of superconformal block decomposition or a
truncation error.

\subsection{The three-point coefficients}

For an all-NS sphere, $C_a(p_1,p_2,p_3)$ is the coefficient of the
normalized holomorphic and antiholomorphic forms $\rho_a$.
Thus $C_0$ is the primary three-point coefficient, and $C_1$
is the coefficient with a $G_{-1/2}$ and an antiholomorphic
$G_{-1/2}$ on the middle primary, in the ordered convention of
the paper. The operator-moving signs remain in the sewing.
Here $C_0$ is real and $C_1$ is purely imaginary on the continuum.

For NS--R--R we denote the reduced SCFT coefficient in the sewing
formula below by $C_{f,\eta}$. The four normalized forms
$\rho_f^{(\eta)}$ do not give four independent Liouville couplings.
With the fixed Ramond ground-state factors kept in the sewing,
\begin{equation}
 C_{1,\eta}(p_1,p_2,p_3)=C_{0,\eta}(p_1,p_2,p_3).
 \label{eq:partition-Cf}
\end{equation}
The two physical equal-family ground three-point values are
$C_{0,+}+C_{0,-}$ and $C_{0,+}-C_{0,-}$.
The label $f$ still distinguishes the normalized chiral forms;
their relative phases are not absorbed into $C_{f,\eta}$.

The explicit coefficients use the standard functions
\begin{equation}
 \Upsilon_{\NS}(x)=\Upsilon_b(x/2)\Upsilon_b((x+Q)/2),\qquad
 \Upsilon_{\R}(x)=\Upsilon_b((x+b)/2)\Upsilon_b((x+b^{-1})/2).
 \label{eq:partition-upsilon}
\end{equation}
These are the only additional special functions needed here.
For $0<\operatorname{Re}x<Q$ their normalization is fixed by
\begin{equation}
 \log\Upsilon_b(x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}
 \left[\left(\frac Q2-x\right)^2e^{-t}
 -\frac{\sinh^2((Q/2-x)t/2)}
 {\sinh(bt/2)\sinh(t/(2b))}\right],\qquad
 \Upsilon'_{\NS}(0)=\frac{\Upsilon_b(b)}2.
 \label{eq:partition-upsilon-definition}
\end{equation}
Analytic continuation outside this strip is fixed by
$\Upsilon_b(Q-x)=\Upsilon_b(x)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Upsilon_b(x+b)=b^{1-2bx}
 \frac{\Gamma(bx)}{\Gamma(1-bx)}\Upsilon_b(x),\qquad
 \Upsilon_b(x+b^{-1})=b^{2x/b-1}
 \frac{\Gamma(x/b)}{\Gamma(1-x/b)}\Upsilon_b(x).
\end{equation}

With the common momentum-independent cosmological prefactor
fixed to one, as in the numerical test, the all-NS coefficients are
\begin{align}
 C_0(p_1,p_2,p_3)
 &=\frac{\Upsilon'_{\NS}(0)}{b^3}
 \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^3
       \sqrt{\Upsilon_{\NS}(2ip_j)\Upsilon_{\NS}(-2ip_j)}}
 {\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\NS}(Q/2+i\sum_jp_j)
       \prod_{j=1}^3\Upsilon_{\NS}(Q/2+i\sum_kp_k-2ip_j)},\nonumber\\
 C_1(p_1,p_2,p_3)
 &=\frac{2i\Upsilon'_{\NS}(0)}{b^3}
 \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^3
       \sqrt{\Upsilon_{\NS}(2ip_j)\Upsilon_{\NS}(-2ip_j)}}
 {\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\R}(Q/2+i\sum_jp_j)
       \prod_{j=1}^3\Upsilon_{\R}(Q/2+i\sum_kp_k-2ip_j)}.
 \label{eq:partition-Ca}
\end{align}
All momentum sums in \eqref{eq:partition-Ca} run from 1 to 3.
For NS--R--R, with $p_1$ on the NS edge, the coefficients for
both $f=0,1$ are
\begin{align}
 C_{f,+}(p_1,p_2,p_3)
 &=\frac{\Upsilon'_{\NS}(0)}{b^3}
 \frac{\displaystyle\sqrt{\prod_{\sigma=\pm1}
     \Upsilon_{\NS}(2i\sigma p_1)
     \Upsilon_{\R}(2i\sigma p_2)\Upsilon_{\R}(2i\sigma p_3)}}
 {\displaystyle\prod_{\sigma=\pm1}
     \Upsilon_{\R}(Q/2+i(p_2+p_3+\sigma p_1))
     \Upsilon_{\NS}(Q/2+i(p_1+\sigma(p_2-p_3)))},\nonumber\\
 C_{f,-}(p_1,p_2,p_3)
 &=\frac{\Upsilon'_{\NS}(0)}{b^3}
 \frac{\displaystyle\sqrt{\prod_{\sigma=\pm1}
     \Upsilon_{\NS}(2i\sigma p_1)
     \Upsilon_{\R}(2i\sigma p_2)\Upsilon_{\R}(2i\sigma p_3)}}
 {\displaystyle\prod_{\sigma=\pm1}
     \Upsilon_{\NS}(Q/2+i(p_2+p_3+\sigma p_1))
     \Upsilon_{\R}(Q/2+i(p_1+\sigma(p_2-p_3)))}.
 \label{eq:partition-Cfeta}
\end{align}
All numerator square roots are positive for
$p_j>0$. The factor $b^{-3}$ includes the
normalized external legs: their gamma-function moduli are $1/b$
per NS leg and one per R leg, and the NS--R--R expression has
the additional relative factor $b^{-2}$. No separate leg
normalizations or three-point symbols are needed.
These formulas use the equal NS/R two-point convention of
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9607120}{Poghossian,
Eqs.~(14)--(15), (51), and (56)}.

\subsection{The block decompositions}

The block functions in this appendix are exactly the paper's
$\mathbf F$, including $(-1)^{K_\Gamma}$. A geometric spin
characteristic and an argument $\eta_e$ of this block are different
labels. In particular, the raw determinant signs in the archived
geometry files cannot be inserted as the lift arguments of a single
$\mathbf F$ with a diagonal absolute-square pairing.

For either theta-channel block, the local factors in
\eqref{eq:partition-local-bpz-factors} give a geometric block. At each
fixed edge-parity triple, the run applies
\begin{equation}
 \left.\mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}\right|_{\epsilon}
 =(-i)^{\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+\epsilon_3}(-1)^{\epsilon_1}
  \left.\mathbf F\right|_{\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:partition-geometric-coefficients}
\end{equation}
The restriction includes the original $q_e$ powers and the paper's
$(-1)^{K_\Gamma}$. The signs $\eta_e$ are fixed independently for each
marked spin; the factor $(-1)^{\epsilon_1}$ is kept in
\eqref{eq:partition-geometric-coefficients} rather than hidden in a
relabeling of $\eta_1$. Coefficients are converted before summing
the block. The all-NS fixed-spin decomposition is diagonal:
\begin{equation}
 \left.Z_{\text{super-Liouville}}\right|_{\NS\NS\NS}
 =\int_{(0,\infty)^3}\prod_{e=1}^3\frac{dp_e}{\pi}|q_e^{h_e}|^2
 \sum_{a=0}^1(-1)^a C_a^2
 \mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(a)}(q;\eta_e)
 \overline{\mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(a)}(q;\eta_e)}.
 \label{eq:partition-allns}
\end{equation}
The $a=1$ phase in the geometric block cancels against its
antiholomorphic counterpart. The factor $(-1)^a$ and the $i$ in
$C_1$ are retained. The direct Majorana state sum and independent
bosonization check below fix the spin assignments. No sum of distinct
spin structures occurs in \eqref{eq:partition-allns}.

For Ramond edges it is also necessary to specify the physical
two-family subspace. In the paper's ground basis its two kets are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{w^+\otimes\widetilde w^+-i w^-\otimes\widetilde w^-}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \frac{w^+\otimes\widetilde w^-+w^-\otimes\widetilde w^+}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:partition-physical-ramond-kets}
\end{equation}
In the product order $(++,+-,-+,--)$, the stored graded product
Gram and the geometric one are, respectively,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{diag}(1,-i,i,-1),\qquad
 \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
 \label{eq:partition-full-ramond-grams}
\end{equation}
Restriction to the same two kets on both sides gives
$\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ for the first pairing and
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ for the second. Thus the first pairing
cannot be inverted on that ket subspace. Choosing a separate dual
embedding can produce an algebraic pairing, but does not by itself
specify geometric sewing. The converted pairing is nondegenerate
on the stated physical states without making such a choice.

The implemented Ramond contraction pairs equal $\eta,\eta'$ and uses
the two-family ground normalization above. After applying
\eqref{eq:partition-geometric-coefficients} coefficientwise, its
fixed-spin contribution is
\begin{equation}
 \left.Z_{\text{super-Liouville}}\right|_{\NS\R\R,\eta_e}
 =\frac12\int\prod_{e=1}^3\frac{dp_e}{\pi}|q_e^{h_e}|^2
 \sum_{\eta=\pm1}C_{0,\eta}^2
 \mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(0,\eta,\eta)}(q;\eta_e)
 \overline{\mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(0,\eta,\eta)}(q;\eta_e)}.
 \label{eq:partition-nsrr}
\end{equation}
Here the weights are $(h_1,\beta_2,\beta_3)$ and the constants have
arguments $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$. The sum over $\eta$ is a three-point-form
sum, not a spin-structure sum. Both $f$ blocks were computed in the
run; at each fixed source spin they satisfy
$\mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(1,\eta,\eta)}
=-\mathbf F_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(0,\eta,\eta)}$ within the numerical
residual reported below. Equation~\eqref{eq:partition-nsrr} uses
only $f=0$. The free-Majorana check tests this geometric sewing;
an independent proof of the interacting Ramond descendant pairing
in this basis remains outside the numerical check.

These are fixed geometric spins, not coherent superpositions of
distinct spin structures. Both formulas use the physical diagonal
spectrum; the implementation accepts independent holomorphic and
antiholomorphic arguments before that specialization.

\subsection{Identity normalization}
\label{app:partition-normalization-resolution}

The relative NS/R normalization is fixed without a higher-genus
calculation. Take the NS momentum to the identity,
$p_1\to iQ/2$, with $p_2=p_3>0$.
After cancelling the common external NS leg,
\eqref{eq:partition-Ca}--\eqref{eq:partition-Cfeta} give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{C_{f,+}(p_1,p_2,p_2)}{C_0(p_1,p_2,p_2)}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{C_{f,-}(p_1,p_2,p_2)}{C_0(p_1,p_2,p_2)}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:partition-identity-residue}
\end{equation}
These compare residues of the common continuum delta function.
Reflection of the Upsilon functions gives the first limit;
$\Upsilon_{\NS}(0)=0$ gives the second. Both physical Ramond
families therefore reproduce the NS identity residue.
An extra half in either reduced coefficient would violate this
condition and suppress a two-vertex partition contribution by four.
This normalization issue is separate from the BPZ conversion above.
The special-function tests at $b=0.8,1,1.4$, including
an independent Barnes-$G$ calculation at $b=1$, are retained
with the results.

\subsection{What must be stated in the paper}

The paper can keep its algebraic Ward identities and recursion, but
must distinguish its stored block from the geometric block used in a
diagonal partition decomposition. For the two theta channels tested here,
\eqref{eq:partition-geometric-coefficients} gives the conversion used.
It follows from converting both inverse Grams and infinity-slot
vertices as in \eqref{eq:partition-bpz-conversion}--
\eqref{eq:partition-geometric-vertex}. A change of tube signs alone
cannot produce it. The numerical evidence here does not establish an
arbitrary-graph partition formula or certify every formula in the paper.

Endpoint interchange requires spin-frame transport. Both the stored
contravariant Gram and the Li--Zamolodchikov Gram are symmetric, not
graded antisymmetric; a nonzero one-dimensional odd-state Gram already
shows why the latter assertion cannot hold. Thus an endpoint-exchange
sign cannot be attributed to a relative minus sign between
$\mathbf G^{AB}$ and $\mathbf G^{BA}$.

Two literal formula corrections should also be retained when transferring
these conventions to the paper: \eqref{eq:graph-sign} defines the
\emph{exponent} $K$ modulo two, and the sign is $(-1)^K$;
the algebraic auxiliary adjoint is $\psi_r^\dagger=-\psi_{-r}$,
with no second dagger on the right. These corrections are distinct from
the BPZ conversion. Neither paper draft was edited for this appendix update.
